FROM WORKBOOK TO COCKPIT

Making operational data useful

This demonstration takes the reporting structure of my Excel sales and shipment workbook and extends it into an interactive purchase, sales and inventory view.

Linked invoice payments, calculated stock balances and replenishment signals are additions built for this portfolio. Every product, customer, order and figure shown here is fictional.

Calculation rules & scope

Stock and value

Opening stock + received purchases − dispatched sales − sample issues = closing stock. Each SKU has a fixed unit cost; inventory value is closing units × that cost. Purchase receipt value is the cost of goods received, not cash paid to suppliers. VAT, returns, transfers, reservations and supplier payments are outside this demonstration.

Stock health

Low stock means on-hand units are at or below the SKU reorder point. Otherwise, more than 90 days of cover, or positive stock with no period sales, is labelled Excess. Cover = closing stock ÷ average daily sales during the selected period. A suggested top-up targets the greater of the reorder point or 30 days of sales, less on-hand units; check incoming orders before acting. These are illustrative planning rules, not demand forecasts.

Receivables and timing

Outstanding = invoices issued through period end − payments received through that date. An unpaid invoice is overdue when its due date is before period end. Collections measure receipts during the selected period, including payments for earlier invoices. Sales and collections therefore need not match. Each demonstration invoice contains one SKU so product filters remain consistent.

Fixed demonstration

July–September 2026, with opening stock on 30 June. All dates use calendar days. Period filters change flows and the closing snapshot; warehouse and category filters apply throughout. This browser-based portfolio example does not connect to an employer system or place orders.
