MSc COURSEWORK · UNIVERSITY OF EXETER

Bayesian inference & classification.

How can we model variation and compare predictions? In MTHM017: Advanced Topics in Statistics, I explored both questions through statistical modelling and machine learning in R.

  • R
  • JAGS / R2jags
  • caret
  • ggplot2

A / MODELLING UNCERTAINTY

Bayesian modelling

I explored 510 reaction-time measurements from 17 participants, transformed the responses to the log scale, and fitted a hierarchical Bayesian model with participant effects and a latent delay component.

Using JAGS, I ran two MCMC chains and examined trace plots, Gelman–Rubin diagnostics and posterior summaries.

B / COMPARING PREDICTIONS

Classification comparison

I explored a two-feature classification dataset, reserved 25% of its 1,000 observations for testing, and compared four model families: QDA, k-nearest neighbours, support vector machines and random forests.

The work combined exploratory plots, parameter tuning and evaluation using confusion matrices, accuracy and Cohen’s kappa.

Skills demonstratedData exploration · Bayesian modelling · Model comparison · Interpreting uncertainty

Methodology, source notes & next steps

Bayesian workflow

Each chain used 10,000 iterations, with the first 5,000 discarded as burn-in. The report includes numerical convergence diagnostics and posterior densities. Some effective sample sizes were low (λ: 180; τ: 320), so longer sampling and completed posterior predictive checks would strengthen the analysis. I present this as a modelling exercise, without clinical conclusions.

Evaluation choices

For kNN, repeated 10-fold cross-validation (five repeats) selected k = 5. The report also tested k = 3. The SVM and random-forest training outputs used 25 bootstrap resamples. Comparing multiple variants on one test set makes the ranking exploratory; a further independent evaluation would be needed after model selection.

Source & interpretation

Based on my MTHM017 Advanced Topics in Statistics coursework report: Bayesian methods and diagnostics on pages 27–34; classification results on pages 45–53. The chart follows the printed R outputs and confusion matrices where prose summaries differ. Models have not been rerun for this page.

What I would develop next

Correct the original-scale posterior summary calculations, complete the predictive checks, and evaluate the classification workflow across repeated splits. The report’s separate clean-label experiment involved retraining and is not combined with the results shown here.
