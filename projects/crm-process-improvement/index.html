The problem behind the maps

Order details, stock movements and payment evidence can sit in different records. When those records disagree, Sales Operations has to reconstruct what happened across Sales, Warehouse and Finance.

These swimlanes make the handovers visible. The proposed process adds named checks, a separate credit-approval route and a clear condition for closing the business step.

AS-IS · RECONSTRUCTED

Where the records can diverge

Open full-size As-Is map ↗

The current-state map follows the order from a customer visit through dispatch, collection and reconciliation. Highlighted steps show reported gaps that need stakeholder validation.

Read the As-Is process in text
  1. Sales — Customer visit. Capture customer and order details.
  2. Sales — Order entry. CRM and supporting records may differ.
  3. Sales — Payment evidence. Share a receipt or transfer proof for the payment checks.
  4. Warehouse / Dispatch — Stock dispatch. Record the stock movement separately.
  5. Warehouse / Dispatch — Delivery follow-up. Receipt and stock ownership may not be reconciled promptly.
  6. Cash Collection — Cash handover, if used. The cash record may need separate confirmation.
  7. Finance — Banking check. Compare bank entries and collection logs; bank-transfer evidence can feed this check directly.
  8. Sales Operations — Reconcile and investigate. Compare order, payment and stock records manually and report variances.

Stock and payment checks run along separate paths. This list explains the numbered steps; it does not imply that every order follows one linear sequence.

TO-BE · PROPOSED V0.2

Checks before closure

Open full-size To-Be map ↗

The proposal separates standard payment from approved sub-distributor credit and assigns checks to the CRM, Sales Operations, the cash receiver and Finance.

Proposed close gate

Complete fields and accurate stock, plus either confirmed payment or valid SD terms, with any exceptions approved.

Standard customers require payment confirmation. For an approved sub-distributor, the business step may close under agreed credit terms while Accounts Receivable remains monitored.

Read the To-Be process in text
  1. 01 · Sales — Visit and sales order. Capture the store, SKU, price, quantity and gifts.
  2. 02 · CRM / System — Validate fields. Check required details and sales or regional stock.
  3. 03 · Sales Operations — Verify order. Review the amount, stock logic and supporting evidence. Flag discrepancies for review.
  4. 04 · CRM / System — Customer type. Distinguish standard customers from sub-distributors.
  5. 05A · Sales — Standard payment. Record bank, cash or split payment and attach evidence.
  6. 05B · Authorised Approver — SD credit approval. For eligible sub-distributors only, approve valid credit terms and a due date.
  7. 06 · Cash Receiver — Cash receipt, if applicable. Confirm the actual cash handover.
  8. 07 · Finance — Finance check. Confirm posted funds or establish the approved SD receivable.
  9. 08 · Sales Operations — Flag exceptions. Keep discrepancies open until reviewed. An authorised approver records the resolution, reason and history.
  10. 09 · CRM / System — Close gate. Close only after the relevant controls pass.

Steps 05A and 05B are alternative routes. Cash confirmation applies when cash is used. The exception route still needs a defined return to verification before implementation.

Supporting design: stock transfers

The third diagram in the editable files separates a warehouse dispatch from a customer sale. The receiver checks the physical SKU and quantity before stock transfers to a region or salesperson; a later customer sale deducts that stock separately. Differences go through review with a reason and evidence.

Stock with unresolved differences should not be treated as unrestricted available stock. Count frequency and negative-stock rules still need management approval.

Before implementation

The maps provide a starting point for requirements discussions. The following work remains to be agreed and tested.

Confirm the workflow

  • Validate the reconstructed As-Is with the people doing each step.
  • Validate how the supporting dispatch and stock-transfer workflow connects to order checks and closure.
  • Define approval permissions and how rejected or approved exceptions return to verification.

Test the controls

  • Agree acceptance cases for bank, cash, split-payment and SD-credit orders.
  • Check that missing evidence, invalid terms or unresolved exceptions prevent closure.
  • Set a baseline for unmatched payments, unresolved exceptions and reconciliation time before a pilot.

The deliverable shown here is the process design. No completed rollout, automated CRM integration or measured improvement is claimed.
